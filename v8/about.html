01HEAL THIS SCENE

One click runs the whole checkup

Heal This Scene scans the scene, applies the fixes that keep your renders identical, turns Relief on, then tunes it on frames it really plays. On Singularity 100_0040 it took 6 seconds.

Shown faster than it runs. Click Again to replay it.

02IT TELLS YOU WHY

Every slowdown comes with its reason

The checkup knows about a hundred kinds of slowdown, from subdivision computed on the CPU to 8K textures filling video memory or a second graphics card sitting idle. It ranks them by what they cost in your scene and explains each one in plain words. About seventy have a one-click fix, and you can undo every fix.

The real list from Singularity 100_0040. Press Fix, then Undo.

03RELIEF

A lighter viewport while you work

Relief lightens the viewport object by object: subdivision above a level, textures when video memory runs short, Material Preview, hair, Grease Pencil effects, and the detail switch of rigs that have one. While the animation plays, heavy rigs stop drawing their bones and steady drivers freeze. Pause Relief and the scene is exactly as you made it.

The readout in the view counts the frames Blender really plays.

Relief in the Frame Medic tab.

04NEW IN 1.1

Ready Frames

Turn it on and Frame Medic computes the heaviest objects of every frame while Blender is idle, then plays them from memory in your viewport. You can still orbit and zoom. Blender Studio's 020_0030 (2,475 objects) plays at 20 to 22 fps instead of 1.5 on a MacBook Pro M3.

Crash Shield

A Remesh voxel size typed too small or a Subdivision level dragged too high can freeze Blender before you can undo. Crash Shield sets the largest value that fits before Blender computes it, and a card tells you. Allow sets the value you asked for.

Drag the voxel size. The real case on our test PC: 0.0001 on a decal, about 106 million faces.
